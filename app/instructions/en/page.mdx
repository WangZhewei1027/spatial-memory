# User guide

**Spatial Memory** lets teams place photos, text, sound, 3D models and scenes on maps. Visitors scan a WeChat QR code to experience the content on their phones and join activities.

## How content is organized

- A **team** owns assets, scenes and activities. Members share access to the team's content.
- **Assets** are reusable images, text, audio, video, models and other content.
- A **scene** combines assets and spatial text into a composition with positions, animation and a timeline.
- A **map collection** organizes placements. The same asset or scene can appear on several maps, with a separate position and display settings for each placement.
- An **activity** provides shop check-ins or visitor messages. It can be linked to several maps, with participation records kept separately for each placement.

A new team can create assets, scenes and activities before creating a map. Collections do not have separate membership permissions.

## Sign up and sign in

New users can register with **Google** or a **mainland China mobile number**. Phone registration requires SMS verification and a password. Google sign-in requires access to Google's authorization page in your browser.

Existing email and phone accounts can sign in with their account identifier and password. New email registration and email password recovery are unavailable. Phone accounts can use “Forgot password” to verify an SMS code and set a new password; Google users continue through Google sign-in.

A team administrator can add existing users or send an invitation link. Open the invitation and sign in to join the team. If an expected team is missing, ask its administrator to check your membership.

## Team workbench

Enter the workbench and select a team. Navigation provides separate **Assets, Scenes, Activities and Map collections** sections. On narrow screens, open the navigation menu.

### Assets

The asset library supports search, type and archive filters, and grid or list views. Creating or uploading an asset does not require a map.

Open an asset to preview or edit it, rename it, make a copy, and inspect its use in scenes, maps or activities. Replacing its file or content affects every reference on the next content fetch.

Archiving prevents edits and new references while retaining existing uses. Restore the asset to use it again.

### Scenes

Create a scene in the scene library or import scene JSON, then open the editor:

1. Add models, images, audio or video from the team library, or create spatial text that belongs only to this scene. Importing a file adds it to the library; add it to the stage separately.
2. Adjust position, rotation and scale on the stage. Use the timeline for entry and exit times, keyframes and media trimming. Audio and video play at their original speed.
3. Open the camera preview to check composition. The viewer position is saved with the scene and used for phone placement; preview pitch and field of view only change the current preview.
4. Save, then open the scene QR code in a compatible mini-program. Unsaved edits do not appear on the phone.

Saving takes effect immediately. Scene positions and dimensions use meters; models are not automatically normalized to the same size. Copying a scene creates an independent composition while reusing its team assets.

If another editor saves first, your save may report a conflict. Keep your input, reload and reconcile the changes before saving again.

### Map collections and placements

Create or open a map from the collection list. Its editor provides a placement catalog, map and detail controls. Select team assets or scenes, then set their positions, presentation and active status.

List selection and map markers stay in sync. Ordinary placements can be repositioned through map interaction. Moving one placement does not move other placements of the same asset.

The map's Activities tab manages activity associations and their status. Edit activity locations in the activity content page; dragging an activity marker does not change its location.

Archiving a collection stops public discovery and placement edits on that map. Team assets and scenes remain available.

## Uploading files

These limits apply to the original file. Your team's settings also determine which types are available:

| Content | Original-file limit | Processing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Images and matching reference images | 32 MiB | Server compression; transparent images retain transparency |
| Audio | 32 MiB, up to 10 minutes | Converted to low-bitrate mono MP3 |
| Video | 512 MiB, up to 5 minutes | Converted to H.264/AAC MP4, up to 640 pixels on the longest side |
| 3D models | 5 MiB | No automatic model compression; phone compatibility requires separate verification |
| Documents | 5 MiB | Upload types allowed by the team |
| Text and links | Enter directly | Save text or a URL without a file upload |

Upload feedback shows validation, transfer, server processing and saving. A completed file transfer still needs a successful asset save. Follow error messages when retrying. Compression changes image dimensions, media quality and audio channels; keep original files for future editing.

Matching anchors use reference images to recognize nearby places. Creating the reference asset, placing it on a map and generating recognition features are separate steps. Recognition does not provide precise camera positioning.

### On-site upload

Choose a team and map within the on-site upload page, then allow browser location access. Capture images, text or audio and place them at the confirmed location. Check the destination map and coordinates before submitting; resolve missing location or map selection using the page prompts.

## Managing activities

Create a **shop check-in** or **visitor-message** activity, then associate it with target maps. New placements start as drafts and become discoverable after activation. Pausing one placement only stops that map's entry.

- **Shop check-in:** one activity contains several check-in points. Enter each point's details, select images and an optional reward image, and set its fixed location on the activity's map. Every associated map uses the same locations and rules.
- **Visitor messages:** set the message length limit and participation area. A limited area uses a center and radius; visitors can submit within the permitted area.

The participation data view filters messages and check-ins by placement. Members with write access can hide or restore records. Progress and messages remain separate across maps; ordinary text assets remain in the asset library.

## QR codes and the mini-program

A team QR code opens the team scope, a map code opens one collection, and a scene code opens an independent scene. Obtain codes from the corresponding collection or scene entry. Admin settings provide a WeChat QR environment selector for release, trial and development versions; the selection applies to the current browser.

Generating a QR code does not upload or publish the mini-program. The phone needs access to the matching backend and a mini-program version supporting the scene's features. Scene codes show the latest saved content; archived scenes cannot be previewed directly.

### Map AR experience

Visitors do not need a platform account. Scan with WeChat, confirm the team and map on the home page, and enter AR. Recent scan history lets visitors switch entries.

Map AR needs location and camera permissions. Look around slowly as nearby text, images, models and media appear. Walking can reveal new content. Ordinary assets are arranged near the camera; distant models use geographic position and direction. This display is not a precision measurement tool.

Matching mode recognizes nearby reference images within a selected map and displays associated content after a match. When activities are enabled, visitors can select a messaging or check-in activity. The server verifies check-in distance.

### Independent scene preview

A scene QR code opens a separate preview without GPS. Once resources and the camera are ready, tap **Place scene**. The saved viewer position aligns with the phone's current position and horizontal direction. By default, the scene origin is 2 meters ahead and 1.5 meters below the phone; this is not a measured ground height.

After placement, pause, resume or restart playback. Leaving, backgrounding or retrying releases current playback resources; reopening requires loading and placing the scene again. The desktop camera preview helps with composition, while the actual phone determines field of view, surroundings and media support.

### Participation and device identity

Check-ins and messages are stored on the server separately for each activity placement. The mini-program uses a locally stored anonymous device identifier to associate progress. Changing phones or clearing mini-program storage does not automatically restore the same participant identity. A failed submission does not count as completed; follow the retry prompt.

## Roles and administration

| Role | Main permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Manage team content, collections, members and invitations; change team settings and manage owners |
| Admin | Manage content, collections, members and invitations; cannot change owners or team settings |
| Member | Create, edit, archive and restore team content and map placements; view the member list |
| Viewer | Read team content |

Member management supports finding existing users, changing non-owner roles and creating invitations. A map selected for an invitation only determines the destination after joining; it does not restrict team content access. Invitation links cannot grant ownership.

Owners manage team settings, including the name, map center, allowed file types and mini-program display styles. Collections are managed in the workbench. Account navigation provides personal settings, language and theme controls; available actions depend on your permissions.
